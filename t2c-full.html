Before you read anything

Every number is labelled with where it came from

Most of this industry's figures are guesses dressed as facts. Each cell here carries a tag so you know which is which.

Filing Straight from the company

Taken from an official filing, results release, or company presentation. As solid as it gets — though companies choose what to disclose and how to frame it.

Reported From credible press

Reported by a reputable outlet but not confirmed in a filing. Usually right. Occasionally not.

Estimate Worked out, not stated

Calculated by dividing one disclosed number by another. Directionally useful, but the two figures often measure slightly different things.

Not disclosed Genuinely unknown

The company has not said. Shown as a blank rather than a guess, because a made-up number is worse than no number.

The one number that matters Delivery rate is switched-on megawatts divided by contracted megawatts. A company with 3 gigawatts contracted and 2% delivered is a construction project. A company with 200MW contracted and 25% delivered is a business. The market usually pays for the first and should be paying for the second.
Contract economics

What each megawatt is actually worth

Two contracts can both say "billions" and be wildly different businesses. Revenue per megawatt per year is the number that separates them.

Why the per-megawatt figures differ so much These are not directly comparable. Some companies rent out bare buildings with power and let the customer bring their own chips — that earns a few million per megawatt. Others buy the chips themselves and sell finished computing — that earns far more per megawatt, but they carry the enormous cost of the chips. Companies also quote different things: total site power versus just the computing load. Always check the business model column before comparing two numbers.
Chips and build cost

What they're installing, and what it costs

The chip generation tells you how long a site stays competitive. The build cost tells you how much money has to be raised to get there.

The cooling split matters Liquid-cooled sites cost roughly three to four times more per megawatt to build than air-cooled ones, because the plumbing is part of the building. The trade-off is that liquid cooling handles the densest, newest chips. A company choosing air-cooled retrofits is choosing cheaper and faster over top-end performance — a reasonable choice, but a different product.
Big investors

Who is buying, and who is backing away

Large US investors must publicly disclose their holdings every quarter. Comparing one quarter to the next shows whether the smart money is adding or reducing.

The catch with this data These disclosures arrive up to 45 days after the quarter ends, so you are always looking at a slightly stale picture. They also only cover long positions in US-listed shares — a fund showing a big stake may be hedged elsewhere in ways you cannot see. Treat a large position as evidence someone did the work, not as a recommendation.

Glossary

Every term on this page, explained

Megawatt (MW) and gigawatt (GW)
Units of electrical power. A gigawatt is 1,000 megawatts. AI data centres are measured in power rather than floor space, because electricity is what limits how many chips you can run. One megawatt roughly powers 1,000 homes, or a few thousand AI chips.
Pipeline
Power the company has secured rights to but has not yet built anything on. The easiest number to announce and the furthest from revenue. Treat large pipeline claims with healthy scepticism.
In build
Sites under active construction with a target completion date. This is where the money is being spent and where delays show up first.
Contracted
A customer has signed for the capacity. Legally binding, but they only start paying once it is delivered and accepted.
Built but uncontracted
Finished capacity sitting there with no customer. Expensive to own and a warning sign if it persists — though in today's market almost everything gets snapped up.
Acceptance
The moment a customer tests a finished site, signs it off, and starts paying. Before acceptance the company has spent money and earned nothing. The most important date in the business.
Delivery rate
Switched-on megawatts divided by contracted megawatts. Our headline measure of whether a company executes or just announces.
Revenue per megawatt
Annual contract value divided by megawatts. Landlord-style businesses earn a few million. Full-stack businesses that buy the chips themselves earn far more, but spend far more too.
Capex
Capital expenditure — the upfront cost of building. Roughly $4m per megawatt for an air-cooled retrofit, versus $10-15m for a purpose-built liquid-cooled site with chips included.
Full stack versus powered shell
A full-stack operator buys the chips and sells finished computing. A powered-shell operator rents out a building with electricity and lets the customer bring their own chips. Completely different capital needs, margins, and risks.
Backlog
The total value of signed contracts not yet delivered. Impressive-sounding, but it says nothing about when — or whether — it converts into cash.
13F filing
A quarterly disclosure large US investors must make listing their shareholdings. Arrives up to 45 days after quarter end, so it is always a little out of date.
Neocloud
Companies renting out AI computing power without being one of the giant cloud providers. Many started as Bitcoin miners and switched, because they already owned the two hardest things to get: land and electricity.
Blackwell, GB300, Vera Rubin
Generations of NVIDIA AI chips, newest last. Newer generations do more work per watt, which is why chip generation determines how long a site stays competitive.
